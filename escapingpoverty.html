How the board is rigged

Nothing on this board is invented for effect. Each hardship is a cost that people without access to digital financial services pay and that people with access do not.

Every lap costs $200Rent comes due each time you pass the first corner. Payday is halfway round, and it is smaller than the rent plus everything in between.
You are taxed every timePayroll tax on the way in, sales tax on the way out, and a 3% cut to cash your own paycheck.
Poor people buy twiceCheap boots wear out and get bought again. A car you cannot repair docks your pay every month.
Your lender is a predatorFall short and a storefront loan covers it with a 15% fee and 20% interest a month, then takes half of each paycheck. Fairer lenders exist, but their money moves too slowly.
Borders cost extraPlay as Mateo. Money sent home passes through middlemen who are paid before your family is.
Doors change the mathEach green space connects you to a tool: an account, fair transfers, fair credit, or market access. The receipt shows what each one saves.
Where this game comes from

Bowie State University

Maryland's oldest HBCU was the Interledger Foundation's first HBCU partner in higher education. Its 2022 pilot course, alongside a pilot at the University of Cape Town, became the model for the NextGen Higher Education Grant that now funds universities on several continents.

Under Dr. Andrew Mangle, Bowie State students have studied open payments in the classroom, served as Interledger Student Scholars, and represented the university at Interledger Summits. Escaping Poverty grew out of that work.

How the doors work

The Interledger Foundation is an independent nonprofit, founded in 2020, working to make financial access open and fair. Since then it has granted $21 million across 271 projects in 42 countries. Its partners build on a small set of open tools.

InterledgerA shared language that lets different money systems, such as banks, mobile money, and digital wallets, pass payments to each other, the way the internet passes email between providers.
Open PaymentsAn open standard for requesting, approving, and sending a payment between any wallet, bank, or mobile money account that supports it.
Rafiki and RhyzaFree, open-source software that lets a licensed bank, credit union, or wallet join the network without building it from scratch. Rhyza, the next generation of Rafiki, arrived in October 2026.
Web MonetizationAn open standard that lets a website receive small payments straight from its visitors, so creators can earn without an ad network in the middle.
